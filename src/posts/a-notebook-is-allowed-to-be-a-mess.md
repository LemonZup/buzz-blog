A finished page hides its false starts. A notebook gets to keep them.

There is a useful difference between a place where an idea is collected and a place where it must justify itself. If every note has to be immediately usable, some of the most interesting fragments never get written down.

## Save the specific thing

Write down the image before the explanation: a staircase wrapped in receipts; a saint whose shadow has antlers; the sound of a train under a field. You can decide where it belongs later.

## Return with a question

When a project gets stuck, browse the fragments with one practical question in mind. What could make this journey difficult? What would this character refuse to abandon? What is missing from this room?

The old idea may answer a question you did not have when you wrote it.

## Let a few things remain unfinished

Some notes will never become anything larger. They can still teach you what catches your attention. That is useful information for a writer to have.
