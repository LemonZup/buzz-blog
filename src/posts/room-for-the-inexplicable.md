A corridor is ordinary until it has one more door on the way back.

That is enough to begin. You do not need a cosmology behind the door. You need someone who notices it, a reason they cannot simply leave, and something worth finding on the other side.

## Make the strangeness tangible

The most useful weirdness gives people something to do. A room that remembers your name can be interesting. A room that opens only when addressed by a name it has forgotten gives the table a problem.

Start with the physical details: the carpet is wet, the lift arrives warm, the photocopier produces a picture of a room nobody recognizes. Let the explanation remain out of reach while the consequences become very clear.

> The players should be able to learn how to survive a place without learning everything that it is.

## Give the impossible a habit

A recurring detail creates a foothold. Perhaps the blue light appears only in reflections. Perhaps every missing person leaves a clean rectangle on the wall. Repeat the detail, then change its context.

- Show the first sign somewhere safe.
- Let a second encounter make the pattern useful.
- Give the players a choice that depends on what they think they know.

A pattern need not become a complete explanation. It only needs to reward attention.

## Leave something unresolved

When the expedition ends, the group might know how to cross the corridor. They might have recovered the object, paid the price, or brought something home by mistake.

The extra door can remain.
